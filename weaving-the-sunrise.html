National Endowment for the Arts Big Read · 2026–27

Weaving the Sunrise

Stitching Stories of Survival with Joy Harjo

A community read of An American Sunrise by Joy Harjo, Muscogee (Creek) Nation citizen and 23rd U.S. Poet Laureate, across Tulsa, Tahlequah, Sapulpa, and Bristow. Readers gather to talk, write, and stitch, and their squares join a shared textile, the Celebration Wall.

Supported by

This project was supported in part by the National Endowment for the Arts.

The book

An American Sunrise, read where it was written about

Joy Harjo's collection returns to the Muscogee homelands her family was forced to leave, and to what endured. Read in eastern Oklahoma, on Muscogee, Cherokee, and Yuchi (Euchee) lands, the poems speak to the places our readers live.

The program frames America's 250th anniversary as a living question rather than a look back: whose voices, laws, and relationships continue to shape the nation. Discussions center stewardship, belonging, memory, and continuity, and pair reading with making, so participants who speak more easily through their hands can still be heard.

The program

From first page to finished wall

All events are free. Exact dates and locations are announced through our partner libraries.

  1. Fall–Winter 2026

    Preparation

    Free copies of An American Sunrise go out through partner libraries, with companion discussions of Braiding Sweetgrass and There There and facilitator training.

  2. Early 2027

    Sunrise Gathering

    Joy Harjo opens the program in Tulsa with a keynote and a call-and-response reading, and stitches the first square of the Celebration Wall. The event streams live to Tahlequah.

  3. Spring 2027

    Stitch & Story

    Book discussions in Tahlequah, Sapulpa, Bristow, and Tulsa combine shared reading, conversation, and optional textile making. A youth writing workshop turns Harjo's themes into "Declarations of Health."

  4. May–June 2027

    Exhibition

    The finished Celebration Wall goes on view in Bristow and then at the OSU Center for Health Sciences in Tulsa.

The Celebration Wall

A quilt the community finishes together

Indigenous quilters create base quilts with open windows. Over the course of the program, squares made by Native elders, youth, medical students, artisans, and neighbors are hand-stitched into those windows, alongside the first square made by Joy Harjo.

The finished wall becomes a companion text to An American Sunrise: a visual record of who gathered, what they read, and what they wanted to carry forward.

Partners

Libraries and community partners

  • Tahlequah Public Library

    Hosts discussions with community meals in the capital of the Cherokee Nation.

  • Sapulpa Public Library

    Hosts discussions on place, naming, and cultural continuity in Creek County.

  • Montfort & Allie B. Jones Memorial Library, Bristow

    Hosts potluck discussions and the first showing of the Celebration Wall.

  • Center for Poets and Writers at OSU-Tulsa

    Author-event expertise and teaching artists for the writing workshops.

  • Office of American Indians in Medicine and Science, OSU-CHS

    Guides Indigenous protocols and connects the program to tribal communities.

  • Youth Medical Mentorship

    Co-hosts the youth writing workshop for high school students headed toward health careers.

  • The Witnessing Project Foundation

    Trained facilitators for the Stitch & Story discussions.
