Research

Two tracks, one question: does the record hold?

Our work runs on two integrated tracks. One studies the infrastructure that carries scientific evidence. The other studies the infrastructure that carries human testimony. Both ask how much of what was observed survives into what is decided.

Track A

Epistemic Infrastructure Science

Meta-research on the systems that move evidence from trial to practice: registries, journals, reporting guidelines, regulators, and the AI tools now entering that pipeline.

  • Harms reporting integrity. Registry–publication comparisons of adverse events across more than a dozen specialties, now funded as a Science-of-Science R01.
  • Trial transparency. Outcome switching, version histories, discontinuation, and non-publication.
  • Data sharing and reproducibility. Journal policies, author compliance, and FAIR principles.
  • AI validation. Journal AI policies, AI trial portfolios, and measurement for automated evidence extraction.
  • Reporting standards. CONSORT-Harms, CONSORT-PRO, core outcome sets, and spin in abstracts.

Track B

Testimony & Witnessing Science

Methods for gathering, preserving, and weighing what patients, communities, and citizens say, and for tracing whether institutions act on it.

  • Public testimony at the FDA. Who speaks at advisory committee hearings, their conflicts of interest, and whether their words change votes.
  • Civic deliberation. Witnessing Before Deliberating, a testimony-based framework for campus civil discourse.
  • Testimony Quilting™ and STITCHCIPHER™. Community testimony methods that pair qualitative analysis with textile and archival practice.
  • Historical evidence. The Battlefield Ledger Project traces how casualty records became modern evidence.

Framework

SAFE Theory 1.0

SAFE Theory treats every hand-off of evidence as a translation with a measurable cost. Its central construct, Representational Fidelity, names four states an observation can occupy as it moves from source to decision.

  1. 01Compatibility
  2. 02Compression
  3. 03Friction
  4. 04Exclusion

      Training

      Students run real studies here.

      The lab is home to the OSU-CHS Office of Medical Student Research. Students join a project team, learn systematic review and meta-research methods, and see their work through to publication. Team leads coordinate specialty projects across our winter and summer cycles.

      • Join

        Medical students at OSU-CHS can join a winter or summer project team. Rotating residents can take the monthly research elective (CLME 9875).

      • Learn

        Training in registry searching, data extraction, reporting guidelines, and, through our NSF CyberTraining pilot, cloud-based computational meta-research.

      • Lead

        Experienced students become team leads who coordinate data collection, mentor new members, and draft manuscripts as first authors.

      • Publish

        Projects are designed for peer-reviewed publication. Most of our 2026 papers have a medical student as first author.
