Witnessing Before Deliberating

A testimony-based framework for campus civil discourse. Before students are asked to debate, they practice speaking from their own experience, listening without rebuttal, and witnessing how a conversation unfolds.

The idea

Readiness comes before deliberation.

Campus dialogue programs usually assume students arrive able to tolerate disagreement, regulate strong emotion, and trust that what they share won't be used against them. Many students don't. Placed into debate before they're ready, they perform or withdraw, and the experience confirms that engagement is pointless.

This project names that the emotional readiness gap and builds an on-ramp to close it. Debate casts people as opponents. Testimony, sharing lived experience without an immediate rebuttal, invites listening instead of defense. Students practice testimony first, then move into structured deliberation, then into civic action.

The Dialogue Lab

Speaker, Listener, Witness

The core of the program is a 90-minute facilitated Dialogue Lab. Students work in groups of three and rotate through every role, so each one experiences the risk of speaking, the discipline of listening, and the distance of observing.

Speaker

Speaks from experience

Answers a testimony prompt, not a policy question, and locates their views in their own life story.

Listener

Listens without rebuttal

Gives full attention with only nods and brief affirmations. No interrupting, debating, or fixing.

Witness

Observes the process

Sits slightly apart and notes how the exchange unfolds: shifts in emotion, moments of connection, moments of withdrawal.

Labs follow trauma-informed safety protocols developed with Let's Fix This and licensed mental health professionals. Facilitators complete a 20-hour certification. The program is educational, not therapeutic.

The Witness Wall

A 70-foot room for many voices

A traveling installation that spends six to eight weeks on each campus, making the range of student experience visible in one place.

Length
70 ft
Height
9 ft
Chambers
3
  • —

    Speaker Room

    A quiet, sound-dampened space where students can record testimony with their consent, under oral history ethics protocols.

  • —

    Listener Room

    Seating for small groups to hear curated, anonymized testimonies from the archive.

  • —

    Witness Room

    A silent space displaying anonymized excerpts for reflection on the campus's collective story.

  • —

    The outside wall

    Students write short anonymous testimonies on fabric cards and attach them. Over the residency the wall becomes a dense record of the community.

The Witness Wall is fabricated by refugee sewing cooperatives in Oklahoma, including RISE Tulsa and Rightfully Sewn.

The pathway

From testimony to civic action

  1. Phase 1

    Readiness

    Dialogue Labs and the Witness Wall build the capacity to speak, listen, and witness, alongside four constitutional curriculum modules developed with the Kinder Institute.

  2. Phase 2

    Deliberation

    Students move into established formats in sequence: Conversation Cafés, then National Issues Forums, then Restorative Justice Circles for those with the most readiness.

  3. Phase 3

    Action

    A Civic Fellows program places students with Oklahoma civic organizations for listening tours, community forums, and public engagement.

  4. Phase 4

    Scaling

    A handbook, facilitator certification, and a national learning cohort of early-adopter campuses carry the model beyond Oklahoma.

Where and when

Five campuses, four years

The same framework is tested across five very different campuses in the Oklahoma State University system, so other colleges can see what it takes to adopt it in their own setting.

  • OSU–Stillwater

    Residential research university

  • OSU–Tulsa

    Urban commuter campus

  • OSU Center for Health Sciences

    Graduate medical and health sciences

  • OSU Institute of Technology

    Technical and workforce training

  • OSU–Oklahoma City

    Open-access community college

  1. Year 1 · 2026

    Development and pilot

    Hiring, partner agreements, curriculum and safety protocols, Witness Wall fabrication, first facilitator cohort, and pilot Dialogue Labs at Stillwater.

  2. Year 2 · 2027

    Multi-campus implementation

    Witness Wall residencies at Stillwater and Tulsa with about 60 Dialogue Labs each. The deliberation sequencing map is finalized.

  3. Year 3 · 2028

    System-wide rollout and action

    Residencies at the Center for Health Sciences, OSU-IT, and OSU-OKC. The Civic Fellows program launches.

  4. Year 4 · 2029

    Scaling and dissemination

    Final toolkit, a national symposium, an Oklahoma Democracy Summit, and transfer of the facilitator certification to NCDD.

Partners

Built with the dialogue field

  • National Coalition for Dialogue & Deliberation

    Methodology and sequencing partner. Leads the facilitator certification, the practitioner handbook, and the national campus learning cohort.

  • Kinder Institute on Constitutional Democracy, University of Missouri

    Grounds the curriculum in the American founding and constitutional tradition, and co-hosts faculty workshops.

  • Let's Fix This

    Co-develops trauma-informed safety protocols and connects the project to the Oklahoma Civic Engagement Table.

  • Listen First Project

    Leads national dissemination through its coalition of more than 500 bridging organizations, and manages the evaluation framework.

  • Oklahoma Oral History Research Program

    Guides consent and archiving protocols for recorded testimony.
