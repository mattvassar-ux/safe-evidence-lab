The Battlefield Ledger Project

Data, Death, and the Making of Modern Evidence

On July 17, 1863, Union and Confederate forces met at Honey Springs in Indian Territory, the largest Civil War engagement in what is now Oklahoma. When the fighting ended, surgeons and clerks began writing down the wounded and the dead. We are bringing those scattered records together, making them searchable, and asking what they captured and what they left out.

The question

When did pain become data?

The Union force at Honey Springs included the 1st Kansas Colored Infantry, Cherokee and Muscogee soldiers of the Indian Home Guard, and Kansas and Colorado regiments. Muscogee and Cherokee men fought on both sides. The medical and casualty records they left behind are uneven. Some entries are detailed; others give a single word, an aggregate label, or nothing at all. Civilian caregivers rarely appear.

Those records now sit scattered across the National Archives, handwritten, untranscribed, and hard to reach without travel and training in nineteenth-century handwriting. Interpretation at the battlefield has centered on troop movements and command. The medical side of the battle, and the documentation practices that shaped it, has gone largely untold.

The SAFE Evidence Lab studies how evidence is recorded, lost, and distorted in modern medicine. This project applies the same questions to the historical record: what was written down, about whom, and how much detail survived.

What we're building

Three public products

  • 01 · Platform

    The Battlefield Ledger Atlas

    A free, web-based platform with searchable transcriptions of about 1,000 pages of field-hospital and casualty records, interactive maps of battlefield medical sites, and historical commentary.

  • 02 · Analysis

    The Omissions Index

    A measure of how completely the record documents each soldier, compared across units: whether a name is given, how specific the injury is, and whether treatment and outcome were recorded. Gaps in the archive become something we can see and study.

  • 03 · Education

    Decode-a-Ledger

    A citizen-transcription program for Oklahoma high school and university students, with lesson plans aligned to state social studies standards on reading primary sources.

How it works

From archive box to searchable record

Every finding is traced back to an original document, and every claim that something is missing is traced back to a documented search.

  • —

    Archival research

    Medical cards, casualty lists, service records, and unit reports from National Archives Record Groups 112, 94, and 393, photographed on site in Washington, D.C.

  • —

    Transcription

    Handwriting recognition with Transkribus, checked by people, and tagged with structured fields: name, unit, injury, treatment, outcome, and level of detail.

  • —

    Mapping

    ArcGIS layers for battlefield sectors, field hospital sites, evacuation routes, and burial grounds, mapped only as precisely as the evidence allows.

  • —

    Consultation

    Review of interpretive content with the Muscogee (Creek) Nation and with descendants of the 1st Kansas Colored Infantry before anything is published.

Where we are

Project timeline

  1. 2026

    Permissions and first archival visits

    Section 106 review with the Oklahoma Historical Society completed. Students began work at the National Archives; Civil War medical cards proved the most efficient way to identify individual soldiers tied to Honey Springs.

  2. 2027

    Digitization and transcription

    Systematic screening of the remaining regimental collections, starting with the Indian Home Guard and the 1st Kansas Colored Infantry, then transcription and data entry.

  3. 2028

    Analysis, mapping, and classrooms

    Omissions Index analysis, GIS layers, the Atlas prototype, a tribal consultation workshop, and the Decode-a-Ledger pilot in Oklahoma schools.

  4. 2029

    Public launch

    The Battlefield Ledger Atlas goes live, followed by a descendant feedback workshop at Honey Springs and an evaluation of educational impact.

Partners

Communities with a stake in the story

  • Muscogee (Creek) Nation Cultural Center

    Tribal consultation on cultural accuracy, historical context, and how Muscogee participation is presented.

  • Descendants of the 1st Kansas Colored Infantry

    Descendant and Buffalo Soldiers representatives advise on how African American soldiers' service is represented.

  • Oklahoma Historical Society

    Section 106 review and stewardship of the Honey Springs Battlefield and Visitor Center.
